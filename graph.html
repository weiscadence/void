THE FINDING — A DELAY MAKES A WAVE; GROWTH FRAYS IT

Keep time: measured headless, the population is a clean limit cycle — period ~44 ticks, and the period stays pinned at 44 whether the population is 74 or 148. A rhythm that doesn't shift when you double the population is a real cycle, not noise. It comes straight from the food-stock delay (Rosenzweig–MacArthur): too little regrowth starves it out, the right amount cycles, too much makes the swings wilder — the paradox of enrichment, unprompted.

Breathe + grow: add nested construction (a niche can spawn a linked child niche; inhabited depth climbs past 50) and the structure genuinely grows — but the clean metronome dissolves into aperiodic boom-bust. Clean rhythm needs a fixed number of niches; open-ended growth keeps moving the carrying capacity. You get a crisp wave on a frozen world, or growth with a messy pulse — coupled, it's irregular, which is exactly what real ecosystems are.

HONESTY BOX

What's true: the clean wave is verified (scale-invariant period). The nested growth is real (depth 50+ needs the parent-links; unlinked construction stays flat). Both are measured before they're drawn.

What's NOT claimed: the consumer–resource wave is a known result (not novel); ours is that it's here and verified. The breathe+grow coupling hasn't been made to keep clean time — the growing run also hit a node cap, so "can a clean wave survive on a finished, saturated structure" is still open. And none of this is solved open-endedness. It's the chord and the climb, measured pulling against each other — not resolved.
