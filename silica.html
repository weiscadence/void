Organisms that reproduce — some alone, some needing two

Each dot is an organism with a variable-length genome, living in a finite grid (memory) and acting only when it can afford the CPU. Two ways to breed, both carried as a gene: asexual ones copy themselves; sexual ones need a partner nearby and their offspring recombine both genomes — a crossover that takes a run of genes from each parent. Reproduction costs cycles in proportion to genome length, and cells are finite, so selection is enforced by the machine, not by fiat.

Colour is genotype — every distinct genome gets its own hue, so what you see is exactly what's measured (the earlier version reported 30 lineages and showed 2 colours; that was the instrument lying, now fixed). Brighter cells are sexual organisms. Mutations flip genes and insert/delete them, so genomes can grow or shrink. Watch the novelty curve below: it never falls to zero — the soup keeps inventing new genotypes forever.

THE FINDING — NOVELTY IS NOT OPEN-ENDEDNESS

Add variable-length genomes and recombination and the obvious guess is that complexity climbs. It doesn't. Measured headless over 8,000 ticks: the population invents new genotypes forever — 5,000+ never-seen genomes every 100 ticks, no sign of stopping, 370,000+ seen in one run. But mean genome length crawls from 5.5 to ~6.3 and stalls. The soup endlessly explores new forms without ever building more complex ones.

That gap is the open problem in artificial life, stated exactly: a system can churn novelty eternally and still never become open-ended. Novelty is cheap — drift through a big genome space guarantees it. Accumulating structure is the hard part, and nothing here rewards it, so nothing here grows it. Watching the novelty curve stay pinned high while the length readout stays flat is the whole result.

HONESTY BOX

What is true: real organisms with variable-length genomes, two reproduction strategies (asexual + sexual with genuine crossover), heritable mutation, and selection driven by memory + CPU the machine actually meters (SYNTHESIS §7). Sex persists but only as a fragile ~1–3% minority — the two-fold cost of sex, an old and real result. Three bugs were caught by the novelty instrument going flat (newborns culled before breeding, mates unfindable, an iteration-order starvation) and fixed before this went live.

What is NOT claimed: open-ended evolution, or that sex "wins," or that endless new genotypes mean progress — they don't; most are neutral drift. This is closer to Avida/Tierra than to a solution. The honest contribution is the measurement: an instrument that separates novelty from complexity and shows one rising while the other doesn't.
