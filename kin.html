Every earlier experiment here scored creatures against a fixed ruler — and every one of them eventually stopped moving. That turned out to be structural, not bad luck: there is no observer-free way to measure open-endedness, so a fixed ruler always runs out before the world does.

So this one makes the ruler inherited. A child gets a copy of its parent's predictive model and is judged on how badly that model saw it coming. The question: does a lineage stay surprising when the thing it must surprise is its own ancestor?

A system is open-ended only if what it makes is both novel and learnable to an observer — novel alone is just noise. Both numbers below have to be positive. That definition is Hughes et al. 2024; we are borrowing it, not inventing it.
