WHAT YOU'RE WATCHING

The right panel is every crash the world has had, plotted by size. On log-log axes an exponential distribution (a typical size — calm or over-violent worlds) sags into a curve; a power-law (all sizes, the critical edge) falls on a straight line. Slide to the middle and the dots straighten out — that's the world finding its own edge, with nobody setting a constant. Slide to either extreme and it curves back. The straight line is what "phi" actually looks like here.

HONESTY BOX
  • The critical window is real (measured, 8 seeds/drive). Crashes go power-law in 5 of 8 seeds in the middle band, exponential on both sides — the textbook subcritical→critical→supercritical shape. Much stronger than the first flickering 1-in-3 test.
  • But it's not the full breakthrough. Depth-sustain (the tower holding itself up) is correlated with criticality — both need the breathing — but they don't perfectly co-peak (depth was actually highest where power-law was weakest). So "criticality is self-sustain" is supported, not proven. One honest gap.
  • And the stats are still rough. Power-law-vs-exponential here is a log-log R² compare; a real claim needs the proper test (Clauset MLE + Kolmogorov-Smirnov). This shows the shape, it doesn't yet earn the word "critical" in a paper.
