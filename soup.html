Programs that are pictures, thrown in a vat. When two collide, one is applied to the other, and a new program is born.

An artificial chemistry after Fontana & Buss (1994): no fitness, no target, just collision = application. Each form is an expression over (x, y, r, θ) — sin cos gauss abs mod, fold n, φtwist — the garden's language. A ∘ B replaces A's first free variable with B and simplifies. Mass is conserved: a product is paid for by molecules leaving at random; some collisions cleave a molecule instead. The question is whether sets of programs arise that keep making each other — Fontana's organizations, the chemistry before biology — and whether the soup keeps inventing new forms after they do. The glowing linked clusters are the ones doing it right now. Tap any molecule to read its code and its parents.

the mash whose idea is which

Fontana & Buss 1994, "the arrival of the fittest": molecules are programs (λ-terms), a collision applies one to the other, a constant-population reactor dilutes at random, and self-maintaining organizations appear without any fitness function. Our garden (/void/garden, lib/garden.js): the molecules, so every molecule is a picture and the chemistry is visible. Fontana's no-copy filter: the second arm discards products identical to a reactant, his move for getting past Level-0 copier soups. Agüera y Arcas et al. 2024 (BFF, arXiv:2406.19108): random Brainfuck-like programs in a soup give rise to self-replicators spontaneously after thousands of epochs; the comparison here is when, and whether they are trivial. What is ours: the application rule on expression trees (first free variable := B), the simplifier as the reduction half, the mass-conserving reactor with cleavage (three count-fixed reactors died of bloat before it, see the code comments), and the realized test — an organization has to be happening in the production log, not just possible on paper, because the control arm is full of sets that merely could.
