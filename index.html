the one line §2
An artificial ecology plateaus for a reason that is a theorem, not a tuning problem. Competitive exclusion: the number of coexisting types is bounded by the number of limiting factors. One resource, one winner. Every substrate here — GP trees, reaction-diffusion, Flow Lenia, self-replicating programs, a graph of niches — hit that wall or one of its cousins. The notebook is the record of lifting each ceiling and naming the next.

When does a world commit to its fate?

Finding 04 left a question open. Worlds built on the open-vocabulary engine either freeze low or climb high, and the split is dynamical, not a capacity limit. So: is a world's fate in its state, and from when? Lenski answered this for E. coli by replaying lines from frozen fossils. We do the same in software — snapshot a parent world at tick τ, spawn replicas that continue with fresh randomness, and ask how often the replicas share the parent's fate at 30,000 ticks.

Pre-stated: ≥0.90 agreement at τ=1,000 is early commitment; crossing 0.90 later is a commitment time; under 0.75 at τ=10,000 is a lottery. Three canaries gate the verdict: fresh seeds must disagree at the base rate (the detector can fire), an identical-twin fork with the parent's exact RNG must reproduce it to the integer (the clone is not leaky), and a menu-capped world must classify as frozen.

What came back (2026-10-18): late commitment, crossing at τ=10,000. The unplanned finding is why. Forks of frozen parents at τ=10,000 came back 38, 38, 38 — identical under different random streams, because nothing stochastic is left. Every frozen world had stopped breathing between tick 3,965 and 9,201, with every exposed niche filled to the one integer where frequency-dependent harvest equals upkeep: 4 / (1 + 18/6) = 1. The "frozen basin" is a quantized fixed point of the crowding law, not an ecological plateau. Finding 04's "dynamical freeze" is now a named mechanism.
